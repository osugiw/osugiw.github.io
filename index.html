01 — About

Background

I'm a graduate student at Rice University, pursuing a Master's in Electrical and Computer Engineering on a Fulbright Scholarship. My work sits at the intersection of RF hardware, FPGA prototyping, and embedded Linux systems.

I currently work as an Embedded Software Engineer on the Houdini Project at Rice's Wireless Lab, building a software-defined radio platform on the RFSoC 4x2 — from custom PLL kernel drivers to multi-rail PCB power design for a phased-array RF front-end.

Before Rice, I spent close to three years as an Embedded Software Engineer at Polytron, Indonesia's largest electronics manufacturer, shipping production firmware for consumer appliances and researching IoT connectivity standards.

Outside of engineering, I've hiked through 15 U.S. national parks so far, with a road trip through the Pacific Northwest, California, and southwest. I document the trips on YouTube, and spend a fair amount of time perfecting pour-over coffee.

02 — Experience

Work History

May 2026 — Present

Embedded Software Engineer

Houdini SDR Project, Wireless Lab, Rice University · Houston, TX

Started as an internship (May to Jul 2026) and continuing as a research project on Houdini, an NSF-funded, open-access, multi-band (CBRS, X-band, Ku-band, V-band) software-defined radio platform for networking, sensing, and imaging research, built on the Xilinx/AMD RFSoC 4x2.

  • Diagnosed an ADF4371 PLL that would not lock on the XUD1A board by tracing the schematic and finding it defaulted to an external LO reference. Fixed it by changing one capacitor to route the internal LO, validating first in a PYNQ Python driver
  • Wrote and customized the Linux IIO driver for the ADF4371 and integrated it into Houdini's PetaLinux build, with test software that confirms PLL lock by streaming ADC/DAC data
  • Established a verified X-band loopback (IF 4.5 GHz, LO 14.5 GHz, RF 10.0 GHz)
  • Designed the schematic for a 4-layer Altium power/control board for a phased-array RF front end (enable sequencing, SPDT switching, current sensing, level translation, ESD protection). A Python netlist check caught six defects before fabrication, and bring-up confirmed the power supply works
  • Fixed the Vivado/PetaLinux build pipeline (port mismatches, corrupted device tree overlays, stale sstate cache)
  • Now benchmarking chirp linearity and wideband chirp bandwidth, and measuring received-signal phase delay at 3.8 GHz and 4.5 GHz as a step toward range measurement
